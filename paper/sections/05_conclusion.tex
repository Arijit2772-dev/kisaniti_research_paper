\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\draftfor{Drafted by Claude from the results above. The authors rewrite this section.}

Selling decisions of an FPO depend on whether the crop's price is set by policy or by the market. For
Paddy (Common) in Punjab it is set by policy: the announced support price was the most accurate forecast, no
learned model beat it, and the useful work is to share the mandi limits fairly. The exact allocation did
that, raised the smallest share a farmer receives from \kn{allpol_p1_nearest_min_fulfilment_pct}\% to
\kn{allpol_kisaniti_min_fulfilment_pct}\% in the synthetic experiment, and recovered part of what a greedy rule gives
up. For basmati the forecast comparison was fixed in advance, and its result is a negative one: on the held-out season no method of eight beat the naive value at 1, 7 or 14 days after correcting for multiple comparisons (Sect.~\ref{sec:results-basmati}), so the baseline is enough for the product. The five stages from data to
recorded sales were built as one system and tested end to end, including an approval that reserves stock exactly
and a way for the manager to send each farmer the message from his own WhatsApp.

\paragraph{Limits.} The mandi index is a market summary, not the price a farmer received. Index days exist
only from September to January, five seasons in all, so the forecast comparison can detect only large
improvements. The cleaned prices have not yet passed the hand check. The FPO in the allocation experiment is
synthetic, with real prices, so no income gain is claimed. The storage sensor was tested on simulated
scenarios, and the node has not run in real grain. Held wheat is not forecast because Punjab mandis report too
few wheat prices outside the procurement window. The system sends nothing by itself: the manager's WhatsApp
hand-over cannot see delivery, and the Punjabi and Hindi texts have not been checked by a native speaker. Off-plan
sales are handled by a provisional rule. No user study has been done.

\paragraph{Future work.} A one-week run of the sensor in grain; a hold-or-sell rule with ranges, evaluated
per season without a significance claim; the allocation comparison with basmati prices and with real transport
costs; a deployment with one FPO after ethics approval, native-speaker review of the messages and a legal review
of the privacy controls; and the official messaging channel, which would let one action reach every farmer.
