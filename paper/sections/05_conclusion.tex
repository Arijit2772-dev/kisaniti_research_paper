\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Whether an FPO gains from price forecasting depends on who sets the price. For Paddy (Common) in Punjab, the price is set by policy.
The announced support price was the most accurate forecast, no learned model beat the last price, and the useful work is to share
mandi limits fairly. In the synthetic experiment, the exact allocation raised the smallest share sold by any farmer from
\kn{allpol_p1_nearest_min_fulfilment_pct}\% to \kn{allpol_kisaniti_min_fulfilment_pct}\%, and it gave up less net return than a greedy
rule. Basmati is sold at market prices, and for basmati the forecast comparison was fixed in advance. Its result is negative: on the
held-out season, none of the eight methods beat the naive forecast at 1, 7 or 14 days once multiple comparisons were accounted for
(Section~\ref{sec:results-basmati}). The naive forecast is therefore enough for the system, although a short-horizon gain for 1121
cannot be ruled out. All five stages, from data to recorded sales, were built as one system and tested end to end.

\paragraph{Scope.} The price index is a market summary rather than the price a farmer received. It covers September to January over
five seasons, so only large forecast gains can be detected. The portal check covers \kn{hc_rows} stratified rows, and there is no second
source against which the portal itself could be checked. The allocation experiment uses a synthetic FPO, so it shows how the method
behaves rather than how much a real farmer would gain, and the storage rule was evaluated on simulated scenarios. Wheat held after
procurement is outside the scope, because Punjab mandis report too few wheat prices outside the procurement season.

\paragraph{Future work.} The next steps are a one-week sensor run in stored grain, hold-or-sell advice that uses the forecast ranges,
and the allocation comparison with basmati prices and measured transport costs. A deployment with one FPO will follow ethics approval.
It will include a comprehension test of the messages with farmers, delivery tracking through a messaging-platform account, a review of
the off-plan sale rule with FPO staff, and a legal review of privacy.
