\subsection{Storage Sensing and Risk Rule}
\label{sec:method-storage}

Figure~\ref{fig:node} shows the storage node and its data path. A protected SHT30 temperature and humidity probe~\cite{sht3x} sits in
a bag of grain and is read every five minutes by an ESP32 board~\cite{esp32}. Readings travel to the server over Message Queuing
Telemetry Transport (MQTT) with Transport Layer Security (TLS). The board keeps a 24-hour buffer and drops a reading only after the
server confirms that it has stored it once. The server assesses the readings every hour.

The rule rests on a simple physical fact. Grain exchanges moisture with the air between its kernels, and the relative humidity (RH) of
that air, divided by 100, is the water activity $a_w$ of the grain. Storage fungi need at least 65\,\% RH~\cite{gwinner1996}, and grain
kept below 65 to 70\,\% degrades slowly~\cite{delucia1994}. The rule takes the median humidity and temperature of the last 24\,h. It
returns LOW for $a_w<0.65$, MEDIUM for $0.65\le a_w<0.70$ and HIGH from $0.70$. The level rises at once, but it falls only after 48\,h
at least 2 points below the limit. The rule returns UNKNOWN, with its reasons, when the probe hangs in shed air, during the first 6\,h,
or when readings are old or missing. It tells whether the air in a bag favors mold and insects; it does not measure moisture content or
detect spoilage. The same firmware runs unchanged on a Wokwi twin with an emulated SHT3x sensor~\cite{wokwi}, and the planner gives
at-risk lots the earliest dates.
