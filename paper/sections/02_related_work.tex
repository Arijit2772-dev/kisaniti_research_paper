\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Price forecasting.} Most Indian studies take daily or monthly AGMARKNET prices and compare one model family
with ARIMA. The largest comparison covers 23 commodities in 165 markets from 2010 to 2024, where LSTM and GRU models had the
lowest errors~\cite{manogna}; adding rainfall and temperature helped deep models on tomato, onion and potato~\cite{nayak}.
Closer to the crops here, Bhoi et al. fitted six time-series models to monthly basmati prices from 74 Punjab markets and
reported forecasts as probability bands~\cite{bhoi2023}, and Balantrapu et al. asked what it means for a series to be anchored
to the MSP: for wheat, a naive forecast was almost as good as their best model~\cite{balantrapu2026}. Graph models
forecast many related series at once~\cite{ozden}\cite{min}; T-GCN and A3T-GCN were built for traffic~\cite{zhao}\cite{bai}.

\paragraph{Storage and sensing.} Measured farm storage losses in North India are small for the first months: 0.39 to
0.50\% for wheat in Madhya Pradesh and zero for paddy in Punjab, where farmers sell soon after harvest, with losses
expected to rise after about three months~\cite{gulati2024report}. Low-cost monitors log temperature and humidity and raise
alarms~\cite{islam}, but none of these systems passes its readings to a selling decision.

\paragraph{Allocation and fairness.} Mixed-integer models already plan agricultural logistics for small
growers, cooperatives and food-grain freight~\cite{urra}\cite{anoop}. They minimise cost or maximise total profit and do not
say how gains are shared. Leximin (lexicographic max-min) methods exist for that~\cite{ogryczak2014}\cite{chen2023fairness} and
have been used to allocate school classrooms with real data~\cite{kurokawa2018}; no agricultural allocation paper read for this
study used a fairness criterion.

\paragraph{Advice to farmers.} Phone advice has a mixed record: SMS price and weather messages to farmers in 100 Maharashtra villages produced no
significant change in prices received~\cite{fafchamps2012}.

Table~\ref{tab:related} lists only what each paper reports. In the 38 studies reviewed, price forecasting, storage monitoring and allocation were studied separately. No study fed a forecast into a plan that shares mandi capacity among the members of an FPO, and none combined such a plan with a fairness rule or a manager's approval before farmers are told. KisanNiti addresses this combination for Punjab, where basmati is the main crop sold at market prices and nearly all reported prices of common paddy equal the MSP.
