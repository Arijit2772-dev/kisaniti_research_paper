\subsection{Paddy (Common): Prices Stay at the MSP}
\label{sec:results-msp}

Table~\ref{tab:msp} describes the \kn{mandis} Punjab mandis that reported in all six seasons: \kn{observed_mandi_days}
mandi-days over \kn{trading_dates} trading dates (\kn{observed_share}\% of those possible). In 2024, \kn{msp_share_2024}\% of the
reported prices were exactly the Grade-A MSP of Rs~\kn{msp_2024} per quintal; the lowest share in any season was
\kn{msp_share_min}\% in \kn{msp_share_min_season}, and in 2024 the mean daily standard deviation across mandis was only
Rs~\kn{cross_sd_2024}.

Seven forecasters were compared on the three latest seasons at horizons of 1 to 7 trading dates: the MSP rule (the announced Grade-A
MSP), the last reported price, a seven-date average, ridge regression, a GRU, T-GCN and A3T-GCN. The MSP rule had the lowest mean absolute
error, Rs~\kn{mae_msp_mean} per quintal, ahead of the last price (Rs~\kn{mae_naive_mean}); the best learned model, A3T-GCN, reached
Rs~\kn{mae_atgcn_mean}, ahead of the GRU (Rs~\kn{mae_gru_mean}) and T-GCN (Rs~\kn{mae_tgcn_mean}). At one trading date A3T-GCN was worse
than the last price on \kn{paired_atgcn_worse} of \kn{paired_dates} dates: a mandi that stays at the MSP is predicted exactly by the last
price, and a network almost never predicts a change of exactly zero. The MSP rule and the last price are close (better on
\kn{paired_msp_better} dates, worse on \kn{paired_msp_worse}, equal on \kn{paired_msp_ties}), and no significance test was run on this
comparison. For Paddy (Common) the announced MSP is therefore used as the price and no forecaster is trained.

\subsection{Basmati Paddy: the Pre-Registered Forecast Comparison}
\label{sec:results-basmati}

The comparison was run once on the held-out season after the protocol and the configuration were frozen (commits
\texttt{C.0 freeze} and \texttt{C.0 configuration freeze} in the repository), on the dated-only index; the portal inspection (Sect.~\ref{sec:method-data}) cannot change the primary result. The index has \kn{data_index_days_1121} days for 1121 and
\kn{data_index_days_1509} for Basmati 1509, because index days exist only from September to January. After the rule that an
origin needs ten index days of its season, the held-out season offers \kn{pairs_1121_h1}, \kn{pairs_1121_h7} and
\kn{pairs_1121_h14} pairs at 1, 7 and 14 days for 1121 and \kn{pairs_1509_h1}, \kn{pairs_1509_h7} and \kn{pairs_1509_h14} for 1509,
fewer than the 60 first planned for 1509, and \kn{pairs_1509_h28} at 28 days, below the 40 pairs needed for a coverage claim. An exploratory power check on earlier seasons puts the smallest detectable improvement at 11 to 17\% of the naive error.

Cleaning choices were measured before any forecast was run. Four outlier screens (past-only median absolute deviation with $k=3$ and
$k=5$, a Hampel filter, an interquartile rule) flag \kn{scr_madk5_flagged_pct}\% to \kn{scr_madk3_flagged_pct}\% of the dated rows, and excluding
them would change \kn{scr_madk5_changed_1121} to \kn{scr_madk3_changed_1121} of the \kn{data_index_days_1121} index days of 1121, by up to
Rs~\kn{scr_iqr15_maxchange_1121} per quintal; the portal inspection cannot decide which are errors. For the inferred dates, \kn{bd_pairs} same-day pairs of 1121 and 1509 in one market (the second row has an inferred date)
have a median price ratio of \kn{bd_ratio_inf} against \kn{bd_ratio_ctl} for \kn{bd_control} own-dated pairs one day apart (interquartile range \kn{bd_iqr_inf} against \kn{bd_iqr_ctl}); only \kn{bd_dups} of \kn{bd_rows} inferred rows fall
on a key that already has another row.

Under the pre-registered rule, none of the \kn{inf_total} comparisons found a method better than the naive value
(\kn{inf_better} better). The largest raw improvement was the GRU at one day for 1121, a change in MAE of
\kn{inf_1121_1_gru_rel}\% (Rs~\kn{fc_1121_1_gru_mae} against Rs~\kn{fc_1121_1_naive_mae}); its bootstrap interval excluded zero, but
the Holm-adjusted $p$ was \kn{inf_1121_1_gru_p}. The only methods that differed significantly from the naive value were worse:
the seasonal naive forecast was significantly worse in \kn{inf_worse_sig} comparisons (Basmati 1509 at one day: change
\kn{inf_1509_1_seasonal_naive_rel}\%, adjusted $p$ \kn{inf_1509_1_seasonal_naive_p}). The result is not that all methods are equal: in \kn{inf_excl_gain} of the \kn{inf_total} comparisons the interval excludes an improvement as
large as the smallest detectable gain, but the other \kn{inf_inconclusive} are inconclusive rather than negative, notably all
\kn{inf_h1_1121_inconclusive} non-seasonal methods at one day for 1121, where improvements of up to about a quarter fit the data (median interval
width \kn{inf_width_median} points). The naive value is a strong forecast for prices that move this little, so the
product uses it with ranges and states that the baseline is enough.

The same \kn{inf_total} comparisons were repeated, with the configuration already chosen, on all \kn{sens_done} index variants declared in the
protocol (inferred dates included; sub-yards merged; at least 2 or 4 mandis per day):
\kn{sens_better} of \kn{sens_comparisons} comparisons found a better method, and only the seasonal naive forecast differed significantly (\kn{sens_other_significant} others).

Secondary and descriptive horizons are reported without a test. At 28 days the GRU and LightGBM had a lower MAE than the naive value for
1121 (Rs~\kn{fc_1121_28_gru_mae} for the GRU against Rs~\kn{fc_1121_28_naive_mae}); both use the day of the season, so this is a hypothesis, not a finding. The partly observed season 2026--27, run separately, is partial: \kn{pro_1121_1_pairs} pairs at one day for 1121 and \kn{pro_1509_1_pairs} for 1509, falling to \kn{pro_1509_14_pairs} at 14 days, so no
test was run. For 1509 the naive value had the lowest MAE at 7 and 14 days (Rs~\kn{pro_1509_7_naive_mae} and
Rs~\kn{pro_1509_14_naive_mae}).

Ranges at 80\% and 95\% were assessed at the three primary horizons. The models' own ranges (ETS, ARIMA, LightGBM quantiles,
Chronos-2) were calibrated in \kn{rng_native_calibrated} of \kn{rng_native_total} cases, too wide in \kn{rng_native_over} and too
narrow in \kn{rng_native_under}. The split-conformal ranges, calibrated on the residuals of the single, more volatile validation season, were calibrated in only
\kn{rng_conf_calibrated} of \kn{rng_conf_total} and too wide in \kn{rng_conf_over}: one season is too little to calibrate a range.

\subsection{Allocation: Fair Plans Against Simple Rules}
\label{sec:results-alloc}

Before any comparison, the solver was checked against a brute-force enumeration that shares only the input types and the exact
objective with it. On \kn{alloc_oracle_instances} random small instances, three back ends (HiGHS, SCIP, CP-SAT) and two leximin
methods, \kn{alloc_oracle_agree} of \kn{alloc_oracle_cases} runs reached exactly the enumeration's complete objective. The method was then compared with simple rules on
\kn{allpol_plans} weekly plans: ten draws of a synthetic 300-member FPO whose lots become ready over six weeks, scored at real 2025
AGMARKNET prices of Paddy (Common) and real mandi coordinates, every plan re-checked by the independent validator. The rules are the nearest mandi, each farmer's independent best choice, a plan that maximises only total net return (utilitarian) or
only the smallest share, a greedy water-filling heuristic, the KisanNiti hierarchy, and the same hierarchy with actual prices (a bound).

Independent choices broke a mandi's daily limit in \kn{allpol_p2_independent_runs_with_violation_pct}\% of the plans, so none was a valid plan. The
nearest-mandi rule respects the limits but left the least-served farmer with \kn{allpol_p1_nearest_min_fulfilment_pct}\% of their lot sold, and
the utilitarian plan did the same (\kn{allpol_utilitarian_min_fulfilment_pct}\%), because maximising the total ignores who is left out. The
KisanNiti hierarchy raised the smallest share to \kn{allpol_kisaniti_min_fulfilment_pct}\%, the most the mandi limits allow, with a Gini
coefficient of \kn{allpol_kisaniti_gini_fulfilment}, at Rs~\kn{allpol_kisaniti_price_of_fairness_rs_per_q} per quintal against the utilitarian plan.
The greedy rule reaches the same smallest share but gives up Rs~\kn{allpol_greedy_price_of_fairness_rs_per_q} per quintal. Actual instead of forecast
prices changed nothing, as expected: prices of Paddy (Common) hardly differ between mandis (Sect.~\ref{sec:results-msp}), so the plan is
decided by distance, limits and fairness. Of the \kn{allpol_exact_solves} exact solves, \kn{allpol_exact_proven} were proven optimal. Three weighted-sum alternatives reached the 300-second limit without a proof in a six-plan pilot and are left out of Table~\ref{tab:alloc}.
The FPO is synthetic: this shows properties of the method, not a gain for any real farmer.

The transport rate and the daily limit are invented inputs, so the comparison was repeated for \kn{alloc_sens_settings} combinations of three
rates (Rs 1, 2 and 4 per quintal-km) and three limits (200, 300 and 500 quintals per mandi-day). The KisanNiti plan was valid in every
setting. The smallest share it secures rises with the limit, from \kn{alloc_sens_kis_min_fulfilment_min}\% to \kn{alloc_sens_kis_min_fulfilment_max}\%, because
no plan can sell more than the mandis can take; its gain over the nearest-mandi rule is Rs~\kn{alloc_sens_kis_gain_vs_p1_min} to
Rs~\kn{alloc_sens_kis_gain_vs_p1_max} per quintal and its price of fairness Rs~\kn{alloc_sens_kis_pof_min} to Rs~\kn{alloc_sens_kis_pof_max}. The greedy
heuristic gave up more than the exact plan in \kn{alloc_sens_greedy_costlier} of \kn{alloc_sens_settings} settings. The nearest-mandi rule left some
farmer without any sale whenever the limit was 300 quintals or less.
Solve times were measured on random instances of 10 to 1,000 farmers (5 to 25 mandis, 3 to 14 days, a 60-second limit, three seeds each).
HiGHS and SCIP proved optimality on every instance up to 100 farmers (median \kn{alloc_scaling_highs_100_median_s} and
\kn{alloc_scaling_scip_100_median_s} seconds at 100) and reached the limit with a feasible plan at 300 and 1,000. CP-SAT proved optimality only
at 10 farmers, because its integer scaling becomes too large beyond that, and the greedy heuristic needed under
\kn{alloc_scaling_greedy_1000_median_s} seconds even at 1,000. Every plan a solver returned was valid. The 300-member persona above was solved exactly in a mean of \kn{allpol_kisaniti_seconds_mean} seconds (maximum
\kn{allpol_kisaniti_seconds_max}); instance structure matters, and larger or less regular FPOs need a time limit and the greedy plan as a fallback.

\subsection{Checking the Manager's Edits}
\label{sec:results-edits}

A manager's edit must be accepted exactly when it keeps the plan valid. A second checker written directly from the rules (admissible choice, no
duplicate line, whole increments, no lot over-allocated or, when the whole lot must be sold, left over, no mandi over its strict limit) gave the
same verdict as the independent validator on \kn{val_agree} of \kn{val_edits} random edits of \kn{val_instances} solved instances (\kn{val_valid} valid,
\kn{val_invalid} invalid, from harmless changes to a single broken rule).

\subsection{The Built System}
\label{sec:results-system}

The system holds \kn{sys_tests_total} automated tests (including parallel approvals on PostgreSQL, isolation between FPOs, append-only records and
exact message texts) and \kn{sys_slices} versioned contracts with \kn{sys_examples_valid} valid and \kn{sys_examples_invalid} invalid examples. A
single command runs \kn{sys_chain_steps} steps on two new fictional FPOs through the interface the website uses (data, stock, a simulated sensor,
plan, edit, approval, messages, the manager's hand-over to WhatsApp, sales, release, a ledger audit and isolation), checks each against an
independent recomputation, and stops at the first deviation; it passed all \kn{sys_chain_passed} steps in about \kn{sys_chain_seconds} seconds, and a
corrupted ledger entry and planted errors (rounding down, a wrong weighted price, a message losing its last character) were each detected. All
farmers, lots and sales are fictional; no user study was done, no real message was sent, and the English, Punjabi and Hindi texts were checked by independent reviewers but not tested with farmers.
