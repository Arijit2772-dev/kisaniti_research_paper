\subsection{Paddy (Common): Prices Stay at the MSP}
\label{sec:results-msp}

\draftfor{Drafted by Claude from the v2 results (Sect.~4.1--4.2 of v2), rewritten to avoid the

Table~\ref{tab:msp} describes the prices of the \kn{mandis} mandis that reported in all six
seasons. Across \kn{trading_dates} trading dates, these mandis reported a price on
\kn{observed_mandi_days} mandi-days (\kn{observed_share}\% of the possible mandi-days). In the
2024 season, \kn{msp_share_2024}\% of the reported prices were exactly Rs~\kn{msp_2024} per
quintal, the Grade-A MSP for that year. The lowest share in any season was
\kn{msp_share_min}\%, in \kn{msp_share_min_season}. Prices also differed very little between
mandis on the same day: in 2024 the mean daily standard deviation across mandis was
Rs~\kn{cross_sd_2024} per quintal.

With prices this close to a policy value, the MSP rule, which predicts the announced Grade-A
MSP for every mandi and date, was the most accurate forecaster we tested. Its mean absolute
error over the three test seasons and horizons of 1 to 7 trading dates was
Rs~\kn{mae_msp_mean} per quintal. Repeating the last reported price came next at
Rs~\kn{mae_naive_mean}. The best learned model was A3T-GCN at Rs~\kn{mae_atgcn_mean}, ahead
of the GRU without a graph (Rs~\kn{mae_gru_mean}) and T-GCN (Rs~\kn{mae_tgcn_mean}).
\authornote{Add the full error table (Table 3 in v2) generated by script, with seed SDs.}

The date-by-date comparison at a horizon of one trading date shows why. A3T-GCN had a larger
error than the naive forecast on \kn{paired_atgcn_worse} of the \kn{paired_dates} test dates.
Whenever a mandi stays at the MSP, the naive forecast is exactly right, and a neural network
almost never predicts a change of exactly zero. The MSP rule tied with the naive forecast on
\kn{paired_msp_ties} dates, was better on \kn{paired_msp_better} and worse on
\kn{paired_msp_worse}; its misses were smaller, so its mean error was lower.
\authornote{No significance test was run in v2. Add Diebold--Mariano tests in v3 (R4)
before writing ``better'' as a statistical claim.}

For Paddy (Common), we therefore use the announced MSP as the price in the selling plan and
do not train a forecaster. We keep the learned models for basmati paddy, whose prices move
with the market (Sect.~\ref{sec:results-basmati}).

\subsection{Allocation: Fair Plans Against Simple Rules}
\label{sec:results-alloc}

The allocation method was compared with simple selling rules on \kn{allpol_plans} weekly plans: ten random
draws of a synthetic FPO of 300 members whose lots become ready over six harvest weeks, scored at real
2025 AGMARKNET prices of Paddy (Common) and real mandi coordinates. Every plan was re-checked by the
independent validator. The compared rules are the nearest mandi on the first free day, each farmer's
independent best choice, a plan that maximises only the total net return (utilitarian), one that maximises
only the smallest share, a greedy water-filling heuristic, the KisanNiti hierarchy, and the same
hierarchy with the actual prices instead of the forecast (a bound).

Independent choices break a mandi's daily limit in \kn{allpol_p2_independent_runs_with_violation_pct}\% of
the plans, so none of them is a valid plan. The nearest-mandi rule respects the limits but leaves the
least-served farmer with a share of \kn{allpol_p1_nearest_min_fulfilment_pct}\%, and the utilitarian plan does
the same (\kn{allpol_utilitarian_min_fulfilment_pct}\%), because maximising the total ignores who is left out. The
KisanNiti hierarchy raises the smallest share to \kn{allpol_kisaniti_min_fulfilment_pct}\%, the highest that the
mandi limits allow, with a Gini coefficient of \kn{allpol_kisaniti_gini_fulfilment}, at a cost of
Rs~\kn{allpol_kisaniti_price_of_fairness_rs_per_q} per quintal against the utilitarian plan. The greedy rule reaches the same
smallest share but gives up Rs~\kn{allpol_greedy_price_of_fairness_rs_per_q} per quintal, so the exact method
recovers the difference. Using actual instead of forecast prices changes nothing, which is expected: the
prices of Paddy (Common) hardly differ between mandis (Sect.~\ref{sec:results-msp}), so the plan is decided
by distance, limits and fairness, not by price. Of the \kn{allpol_exact_solves} exact solves,
\kn{allpol_exact_proven} were proven optimal. Three weighted-sum alternatives were also tried in a pilot of six
plans; all reached the 300-second time limit without a proof of optimality and are therefore left out of
Table~\ref{tab:alloc}. The experiment uses a synthetic FPO, so it shows properties of the method and not a
gain for any real farmer; the comparison with basmati prices, where mandis differ, is future work.

\subsection{The Built System}
\label{sec:results-system}

The five stages were built as one product and tested together. The backend, the shared algorithms, the sensor
ingestion and the interface contracts hold \kn{sys_tests_total} automated tests (backend \kn{sys_tests_backend},
core \kn{sys_tests_core}, ingestion \kn{sys_tests_ingestion}, contract examples \kn{sys_tests_contracts}), which
include parallel approvals on PostgreSQL, isolation between two FPOs, append-only records and the exact
rendering of every message text. The interface is fixed by \kn{sys_slices} versioned contracts with
\kn{sys_examples_valid} valid and \kn{sys_examples_invalid} invalid examples that every change must still satisfy. A single command
runs \kn{sys_chain_steps} steps on two new fictional FPOs through the same interface the website uses: data,
stock, a simulated storage sensor, plan, edit, approval, messages, the manager's hand-over to WhatsApp, sales, release,
a ledger audit and isolation. Each step is checked against an independent recomputation of the amounts, the
rounding, the stock ledger and the message texts, and the run stops at the first deviation. A full run takes about
\kn{sys_chain_seconds} seconds and passed all \kn{sys_chain_passed} steps. To show that the run can fail, a
corrupted ledger entry and deliberately planted errors (rounding down, a wrong weighted price, a message
that loses its last character) were each detected. All farmers, lots and sales in these runs are fictional. No
user study has been done, no real message was sent, and the Punjabi and Hindi texts have not yet been read by a native speaker.
