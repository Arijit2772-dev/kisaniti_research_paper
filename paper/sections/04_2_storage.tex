\subsection{Storage Rule on Simulated Scenarios}
\label{sec:results-storage}

\draftfor{Claude draft, 22 Oct 2026. All readings in this subsection are simulated. Add the real-node results
(salt offset, settling time, outage tests, one-week grain run) when the kit is built (\texttt{firmware/BUILD\_GUIDE.md}).}

We have not yet run the node in real grain, so we tested the rule on simulated sensor streams for basmati paddy
stored on a farm near Patiala. The streams follow the monthly temperature and humidity normals for Patiala for
1991--2020; shed air swings by $\pm15$ points of relative humidity each day; the grain's temperature follows the shed
air with a 2-day lag; and its water activity drifts towards the shed's daily mean humidity, slowly, as it would through
a jute bag. These are assumptions, listed in \texttt{layers/L3b\_storage\_iot/scenarios.py}.
\authornote{Cite the IMD 1991--2020 normals document directly; the values were read from a secondary table.}
The \kn{stor_scenarios} scenarios cover dry grain held from November to February, grain held into the monsoon, a lot
probed straight after harvest and sun-dried on day~8, the monsoon case with Wi-Fi and power outages, a probe left
hanging in shed air, and grain sitting at $a_w\approx0.65$. Each ran with \kn{stor_seeds} noise seeds, with no sensor
bias and with a bias of $\pm2$\,\%RH and $\pm0.3$\,$^\circ$C.

We compared STORAGE-DEMO-0.1 with three simpler rules: the same thresholds on the latest reading only (no median, no
hysteresis), the same thresholds on shed-air humidity, and temperature bands alone. Each rule is scored against the
category that the rule's definition gives for the noise-free grain values at the same hour. This measures how much
noise, outages, placement and short cuts move a rule away from that definition. It does not show that the definition
predicts spoilage.

Table~\ref{tab:storage_rules} and Fig.~\ref{fig:storage} give the results. The largest difference was stability.
Without sensor bias, STORAGE-DEMO-0.1 changed category at most \kn{stor_demo_flips_max} times per week in any
scenario. The shed-air rule changed \kn{stor_shed_flips_min}--\kn{stor_shed_flips_max} times per week, following the
daily humidity cycle rather than the grain. During the dry winter hold it marked \kn{stor_s1_shed_high}\,\% of hours
HIGH, while the grain's water activity never exceeded \kn{stor_s1_grain_aw_max}. Temperature alone marked \kn{stor_s2_temp_high}\,\% of the
monsoon hold HIGH, because warm weather says nothing about moisture. When the probe hung in shed air, our rule returned
UNKNOWN for every hour, as intended.

The median and the start-up checks have a cost. A lot probed straight after harvest was marked HIGH
\kn{stor_s3_demo_highdelay}\,h after its true condition, because the first day after insertion is UNKNOWN; in the
monsoon hold, the 24-hour median delayed HIGH by about \kn{stor_s2_demo_highdelay}\,h. With the outages, the rule was
UNKNOWN for \kn{stor_s4_demo_unknown}\,\% of the hours, against \kn{stor_s4_instant_unknown}\,\% for the latest-reading
rule; \kn{stor_s4_lost_samples} readings were lost, half to the buffer limit during a 30-hour Wi-Fi outage and half to a
6-hour power cut. For grain sitting near $a_w=0.65$, hysteresis held MEDIUM: no changes, but only
\kn{stor_s6_demo_agree}\,\% agreement, against \kn{stor_s6_instant_agree}\,\% for the latest-reading rule, which changed
category many times a week. The rule leans to the cautious level in this case.

Sensor bias mattered as much as the choice of rule. With $+2$\,\%RH, the rule stayed at MEDIUM for most of the weeks
after the fresh lot was dried (agreement \kn{stor_s3_demo_agree_biasplus}\,\%), because the 2-point margin for falling
back equals the sensor's tolerance. With $-2$\,\%RH it missed \kn{stor_s2_demo_missedhigh_biasminus}\,\% of the HIGH
hours in the monsoon hold, as did the latest-reading rule. We therefore treat the salt calibration (75.3\,\%RH over
saturated NaCl at 25\,$^\circ$C~\cite{greenspan1977}) as a required step before real readings are interpreted.
