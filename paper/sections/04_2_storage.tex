\subsection{Storage Rule on Simulated Scenarios}
\label{sec:results-storage}

\draftfor{Claude draft, 22 Oct 2026. All readings in this subsection are simulated. Add the real-node results
(salt offset, settling time, outage tests, one-week grain run) when the kit is built (\texttt{firmware/BUILD\_GUIDE.md}).}

We have not yet run the node in real grain, so we tested the rule on simulated sensor streams for basmati paddy
stored on a farm near Patiala. Air temperature follows the monthly mean daily maximum and minimum of the IMD station
at Patiala for 1991--2020\authornote{Cite the IMD 1991--2020 climate normals for Patiala: no verified reference on file yet.}. Shed humidity follows a daily cycle fitted through IMD's two monthly means,
taken at 08:30 and 17:30 IST; we assume shed air equals outdoor air. The grain's temperature follows the shed air with a
2-day lag, and its water activity drifts slowly towards the shed's daily mean humidity, as it would through a jute bag.
These are assumptions, listed in \texttt{layers/L3b\_storage\_iot/scenarios.py}. The \kn{stor_scenarios} scenarios
cover grain stored dry and held from November to February, grain held into the monsoon, a lot probed straight after
harvest and sun-dried on day~8, the monsoon case with Wi-Fi and power outages, a probe left hanging in shed air, and
grain sitting at $a_w\approx0.65$. Each ran with \kn{stor_seeds} noise seeds, with no sensor bias and with a bias of
$\pm2$\,\%RH and $\pm0.3$\,$^\circ$C. Patiala's winter mornings are humid (mean RH at 08:30 of 84\,\% or more from November
to January), so in our model even grain stored dry reached $a_w=\kn{stor_s1_grain_aw_max}$ by February; how fast this
happens in a real shed depends on the bag and the shed, which we have not measured.

We compared STORAGE-DEMO-0.1 with three simpler rules: the same thresholds on the latest reading only (no median, no
hysteresis), the same thresholds on shed-air humidity, and temperature bands alone. Each rule is scored against the
category that the rule's definition gives for the noise-free grain values at the same hour. This measures how much
noise, outages, placement and short cuts move a rule away from that definition. It does not show that the definition
predicts spoilage.

Table~\ref{tab:storage_rules} and Fig.~\ref{fig:storage} give the results. Without sensor bias, STORAGE-DEMO-0.1
agreed with the intended category on \kn{stor_s3_demo_agree}--\kn{stor_s2_demo_agree}\,\% of known hours in the four
scenarios with the probe in grain and moving conditions, and changed category at most \kn{stor_demo_flips_max} times
per week. The latest-reading rule agreed about as often (\kn{stor_instant_agree_min}--\kn{stor_instant_agree_max}\,\%)
but changed \kn{stor_instant_flips_min}--\kn{stor_instant_flips_max} times per week, mostly when the grain sat close to
a limit. A shed-air reading was a poor stand-in for the grain: it agreed on \kn{stor_shed_agree_min}--%
\kn{stor_shed_agree_max}\,\% of hours and changed \kn{stor_shed_flips_min}--\kn{stor_shed_flips_max} times per week,
following the daily humidity cycle. Temperature alone marked \kn{stor_s2_temp_high}\,\% of the monsoon hold HIGH and
gave no HIGH hour in the winter hold, when moisture rather than heat was the problem (agreement
\kn{stor_s1_temp_agree}\,\%). When the probe hung in shed air, our rule returned UNKNOWN for every hour, as intended.

The median and the start-up checks have a cost. In the winter, monsoon and outage scenarios the 24-hour median
reported HIGH about \kn{stor_s2_demo_highdelay}\,h after the intended category. A lot probed straight after harvest
was marked HIGH \kn{stor_s3_demo_highdelay}\,h late, because the first day after insertion is UNKNOWN, and after it
was dried the rule stayed HIGH for two more days, which made \kn{stor_s3_demo_falsehigh}\,\% of its known hours a false
HIGH. With the outages, the rule was UNKNOWN for \kn{stor_s4_demo_unknown}\,\% of the hours, against
\kn{stor_s4_instant_unknown}\,\% for the latest-reading rule; \kn{stor_s4_lost_samples} readings were lost, half to the
buffer limit during a 30-hour Wi-Fi outage and half to a 6-hour power cut. For grain sitting at $a_w\approx0.65$,
hysteresis held MEDIUM with no changes, which agreed with the intended category on only
\kn{stor_s6_demo_agree}\,\% of hours; the latest-reading rule agreed on \kn{stor_s6_instant_agree}\,\% but changed
\kn{stor_s6_instant_flips} times per week. Here the rule keeps the cautious level.

A $\pm2$\,\%RH sensor error mattered about as much as the choice of rule. With $+2$\,\%RH, agreement in the four
in-grain scenarios fell to \kn{stor_demo_agree_biasplus_min}--\kn{stor_demo_agree_biasplus_max}\,\% and up to
\kn{stor_demo_falsehigh_biasplus_max}\,\% of hours were a false HIGH; with $-2$\,\%RH it fell to
\kn{stor_demo_agree_biasminus_min}--\kn{stor_demo_agree_biasminus_max}\,\% and up to
\kn{stor_demo_missedhigh_biasminus_max}\,\% of hours were a missed HIGH. The latest-reading rule was affected in the
same way, so the median does not protect against bias. We therefore treat the salt calibration (75.3\,\%RH over
saturated NaCl at 25\,$^\circ$C~\cite{greenspan1977}) as a required step before real readings are interpreted.
