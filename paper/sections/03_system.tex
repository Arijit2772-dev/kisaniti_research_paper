\section{System Design and Methods}
\label{sec:system}

KisanNiti follows the order in which an FPO manager works (Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}): (1)~farmer records, mandi prices
and storage readings are collected and cleaned; (2)~a forecast, a storage assessment and a fair allocation produce a proposed
plan; (3)~the manager reviews, edits and approves or rejects it; (4)~each farmer is told in their own language; and
(5)~replies and actual sales are recorded and compared with the plan. Five rules hold throughout: raw facts, derived values, proposals, human decisions and what happened are stored separately; quantities and
money are exact (grams and paise, never floating point); each FPO sees only its own data, enforced in the query and again in
the database; every change is an append-only record with its author; and every failure is explicit, so a missing contact,
consent or language blocks a message with a stated reason instead of being guessed.

\subsection{Stage 1: Data and Cleaning}
\label{sec:method-data}

Prices come from the AGMARKNET report \emph{Date Wise Prices for Specified Commodity}, downloaded by hand each month because the portal
asks for a CAPTCHA. \kn{data_manifests} months of Paddy (Basmati) for Punjab are filed with checksums. A parser keeps every line with its
source file and position, so each cleaned row traces to its source; the files hold \kn{data_rows_total} rows (\kn{data_rows_dated} dated,
\kn{data_rows_blank} without a date) and every row is accounted for. Mechanical checks flag suspicious rows but remove none. Where the
portal shows a variety under the date of the row above, the date is inferred and flagged. Prices of one variety become a daily index, the
median modal price over the mandis that reported the variety that day (at least three, no filling); it is a market summary, not the price a
farmer received. Farmer records (members, lots with exact quantity, an append-only stock ledger) are entered by staff, and sensor readings
are assessed hourly (Sect.~\ref{sec:method-storage}). The cleaned release is imported with an exact row reconciliation and the status
\texttt{PENDING\_HAND\_CHECK}; it becomes \texttt{VALIDATED} only on the owner's written sign-off after a hand check of \kn{data_handcheck_rows} stratified rows against the
portal. Software agents inspected them on the live portal (owner entered the CAPTCHAs; sign-off pending): all \kn{hc_modal} modal prices
agree, \kn{hc_numeric} rows agree fully, \kn{hc_arr_differ} differ in arrivals and \kn{hc_missing_zero} show a missing bound as 0.00; a second agent re-read \kn{hc_repl} rows, \kn{hc_repl_same} identical. The sample is purposive, not an error rate. The page splits a date total over variety rows, equally or not, so the meaning of exported arrivals is left open (the index uses modal prices).
\kn{hc_inferred_ok} of \kn{hc_inferred} inferred-date rows appear beneath the preceding date with the same prices; no merged cell is in the markup.

\subsection{Stage 2: Forecasting, Storage Risk and Fair Allocation}
\label{sec:method-forecast}

\paragraph{Regime test.} For each crop and season, the share of reported prices equal to the announced MSP is computed. A high
share marks a price-bound crop, for which the MSP is used as the price and no forecaster is trained.

\paragraph{Pre-registered forecast comparison.} For basmati (1121 and 1509) the index is forecast at 1, 7 and 14 calendar days (28 secondary,
60 descriptive). Seasons 2021--22 to 2023--24 give the initial training, 2024--25 is used to tune and calibrate, and 2025--26 is held out and
evaluated once; September and October 2026 are a separate partial check. Each forecast uses only data up to its origin day, and an origin
needs ten index days of its season. Eight methods were fixed beforehand: the last value, a seasonal naive value, a five-day average, ETS, ARIMA,
LightGBM, a small GRU and the pretrained Chronos-2 model~\cite{ansari2024chronos} used zero-shot, with 80\% and 95\% ranges from analytic intervals, quantile objectives, split-conformal residuals
or the model's quantiles. Accuracy is the MAE in rupees per quintal and MASE. Each model is compared with the naive value by a
Diebold--Mariano test with the Harvey--Leybourne--Newbold correction, Holm-corrected over all 42 comparisons, with a block-bootstrap interval of
the relative change in MAE. A model counts as better only if the adjusted $p<0.05$, the interval excludes zero and the gain exceeds the smallest
the sample can detect (13.3\% for 1121, 17.2\% for 1509); otherwise the product keeps the naive value. The protocol and its choices were frozen before the run, the code is tested on synthetic series, and a gate in the runner refuses any run before
the freeze, on changed code or data, or a second test run.

\paragraph{Fair allocation.} A plan gives each lot a mandi, a day and a quantity, and each mandi has a daily limit shared by all
members. An exact mixed-integer program with a fixed hierarchy allocates as much as possible, then maximises the smallest share any
farmer receives (and so on, lexicographically), then gives lots at risk of spoilage the earliest dates, then maximises net return,
then breaks ties deterministically. An independent checker re-validates every plan, and a brute-force reference confirms the solver
on small cases.

\subsection{Stages 3 to 5: Review, Messages, Sales}
\label{sec:method-approval}

A proposed plan is stored unchanged. A manager's edit becomes a new revision, accepted only if the independent validator finds no violation.
Approval needs the approve permission and an acknowledgement of every warning, and in one database transaction records the decision and
reserves exact quantities in the stock ledger; a reservation cannot exceed free stock, approval cannot be given twice, and two plans competing
for one lot cannot both succeed (tested with parallel requests on PostgreSQL). After approval, one message per farmer is built from a
versioned template in the farmer's language (Punjabi, Hindi or English) and the approved facts only, with no price and no promise. A message
needs an active farmer with a phone number and consent to WhatsApp messages, rechecked at sending; a preview channel shows the exact text and
sends nothing. Without a messaging-platform account, the manager presses ``Send to farmers'', confirms with yes or no, and receives one link
per farmer that opens his own WhatsApp with the text written; he sends it there and reports ``sent'' or ``not sent''. The system cannot see
delivery, so that report is stored as his statement, never as a delivery. Replies are entered by staff and never read as acceptance. Sales are
entered from the paper J-form with exact amounts (ties to even), duplicate form numbers refused and mistakes voided by compensating records; a
sale on plan consumes its reservation, and an off-plan sale needs the person to choose which reservation to give up (a provisional rule). The
system reports planned against actual quantity, price and share per farmer.
