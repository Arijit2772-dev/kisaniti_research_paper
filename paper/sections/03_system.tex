\section{System Design and Methods}
\label{sec:system}

Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} shows the KisanNiti architecture. It follows the order in which an FPO manager works. (1)~Farmer records,
mandi prices and storage readings are collected and cleaned. (2)~A forecast, a storage assessment and a fair allocation produce a
proposed plan. (3)~The manager reviews the plan, edits it, and approves or rejects it. (4)~Each farmer is told about the plan in their
own language. (5)~Replies and actual sales are recorded and compared with the plan.

Five design rules apply at every stage. Raw facts, derived values, proposals, human decisions and actual outcomes are stored separately.
Quantities and money are kept exact, in grams and paise, and never as floating-point numbers. Each FPO sees only its own data, which is
enforced both in the queries and in the database. Every change is an append-only record that names its author. Every failure is
explicit: a missing contact, consent or language blocks a message with a stated reason, and nothing is guessed.

\subsection{Stage 1: Data and Cleaning}
\label{sec:method-data}

Prices come from the AGMARKNET report \emph{Date Wise Prices for Specified Commodity}~\cite{agmarknet}. The portal asks for a Completely Automated
Public Turing test to tell Computers and Humans Apart (CAPTCHA), so the files were downloaded by hand, one month at a time,
and \kn{data_manifests} monthly files of Paddy (Basmati) for Punjab are stored with checksums. The parser records the source file and
line of every row, so each cleaned row can be traced back to the portal. The files hold \kn{data_rows_total} rows, \kn{data_rows_dated}
with a date and \kn{data_rows_blank} without one, and every row is accounted for. Automatic checks flag suspicious rows but remove none.
Where the portal lists a variety under the date of the row above, the date is inferred and flagged.

For each variety, the daily index is the median modal price over the mandis that reported the variety that day. At least three mandis
are needed, and missing days are not filled in. The index summarizes the market; it is not the price that any one farmer received.
Farmer records, namely members, lots with exact quantities and an append-only stock ledger, are entered by FPO staff. Sensor readings
are assessed every hour (Section~\ref{sec:method-storage}).

The cleaned data enter the system marked as pending. They are marked as validated only after the authors sign off a hand check against
the portal. For this check, \kn{data_handcheck_rows} stratified rows were read on the live portal by automated browser scripts, with the
CAPTCHAs entered by the authors. All \kn{hc_modal} modal prices agreed. In \kn{hc_numeric} rows every field agreed, \kn{hc_arr_differ}
rows differed only in arrivals, and \kn{hc_missing_zero} showed a missing bound as 0.00. An independent second pass re-read
\kn{hc_repl} rows and found \kn{hc_repl_same} of them identical. The sample was chosen on purpose, so it does not estimate an error rate.
The portal splits the total arrivals of a date over the variety rows, not always equally, which makes exported arrivals hard to
interpret; for this reason the index uses modal prices only. Of the \kn{hc_inferred} rows with an inferred date, \kn{hc_inferred_ok}
appear on the portal beneath the preceding date with the same prices, and the page markup contains no merged cells.

\subsection{Stage 2: Forecasting, Storage Risk and Fair Allocation}
\label{sec:method-forecast}

\paragraph{Regime test.} For each crop and season, the share of reported prices equal to the announced MSP is computed. A high share
marks a price-bound crop. For such a crop the MSP is used as the price, and no forecaster is trained.

\paragraph{Pre-registered forecast comparison.} Basmati is studied through two varieties, Pusa Basmati 1121 and Pusa Basmati 1509
(called 1121 and 1509 below). Their index is forecast 1, 7 and 14 calendar days ahead. A 28-day horizon is a secondary outcome, and
60 days is reported only descriptively. The seasons are used in time order: 2021--22 to 2023--24 for initial training, 2024--25 for
tuning and calibration, and 2025--26 as the held-out season, evaluated once. September and October 2026 form a separate, partial check.
Each forecast uses only data up to its origin day, and an origin needs ten index days of its season.

The eight methods were fixed beforehand. Three are simple: the last value (the naive forecast), a seasonal naive value and a five-day
average. The other five are exponential smoothing (ETS), ARIMA, the light gradient-boosting machine (LightGBM), a small GRU, and the
pretrained Chronos-2 model~\cite{ansari2024chronos} used zero-shot. Every method also gives 80\% and 95\% ranges, taken from analytic
intervals, quantile objectives, split-conformal residuals or the model's own quantiles.

Accuracy is measured by the mean absolute error (MAE) in Rs per quintal and by the mean absolute scaled error (MASE). Each method is
compared with the naive forecast by a Diebold--Mariano test with the Harvey--Leybourne--Newbold correction. The $p$-values are
Holm-corrected over all 42 comparisons, and a block bootstrap gives an interval for the relative change in MAE. A method counts as
better only if three conditions hold: the adjusted $p<0.05$, the interval excludes zero, and the gain is larger than the smallest gain
the sample can detect (13.3\% for 1121 and 17.2\% for 1509). Otherwise the system keeps the naive forecast. The protocol was frozen
before the run, and the code was first tested on synthetic series. The runner refuses to start before the freeze, after any change to
the code or data, or for a second test run. Figure~\ref{fig:protocol} summarizes the protocol from the price files to the decision rule.

\paragraph{Fair allocation.} A plan gives each lot a mandi, a day and a quantity. Each mandi has a daily limit that all members share.
An exact mixed-integer program works through a fixed order of goals. It first sells as much as possible. It then raises the smallest
share sold by any farmer, then the next smallest, and so on, in leximin order. After that, lots at risk of spoilage get the earliest
dates, net return is maximized, and any remaining ties are broken deterministically. An independent checker re-validates every plan,
and on small cases a brute-force search confirms the solver's answer.

\subsection{Stages 3 to 5: Review, Messages, Sales}
\label{sec:method-approval}

A proposed plan is stored unchanged. When the manager edits it, the edit becomes a new revision, which is accepted only if the
independent validator finds no violation. Approval needs the approve permission and an acknowledgement of every warning. It runs in a
single database transaction that records the decision and reserves the exact quantities in the stock ledger. A reservation cannot
exceed free stock, a plan cannot be approved twice, and when two plans compete for the same lot only one can succeed. These guarantees
were tested with parallel requests on PostgreSQL.

After approval, one message per farmer is built from a versioned template in the farmer's language (Punjabi, Hindi or English). It
contains only the approved facts, with no price and no promise. A message is prepared only for an active farmer with a phone number
and consent to WhatsApp messages, and consent is checked again at sending. A preview channel shows the exact text without sending
anything. Because the FPO has no messaging-platform account, sending is handed to the manager. The manager presses ``Send to
farmers'', confirms, and receives one link per farmer. Each link opens the manager's own WhatsApp with the text already written; the
manager sends it there and reports it as sent or not sent. The system cannot see delivery, so this report is stored as the manager's
statement and not as a delivery. Farmers' replies are entered by staff and are never read as acceptance.

Sales are entered from the paper J-form, the sale receipt issued in Punjab mandis, with exact amounts rounded half to even. Duplicate
form numbers are refused, and mistakes are voided by compensating records. A sale that follows the plan consumes its reservation. For
an off-plan sale, the person entering it must choose which reservation to give up. The system then reports planned against actual
quantity, price and share for each farmer.
