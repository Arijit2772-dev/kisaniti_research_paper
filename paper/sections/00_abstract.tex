\begin{abstract}
A Farmer Producer Organization has to decide where, when and how much of its members' produce to sell, yet price forecasting,
storage monitoring and logistics are usually handled by separate tools. KisanNiti brings them together in the order a manager works:
prices and farmer records are collected and cleaned, a selling plan is proposed and reviewed, each farmer is told in their own
language, and the actual sales are recorded. In Punjab, at least \kn{msp_share_min}\% of the Paddy (Common) prices reported in every
season from \kn{season_first} to \kn{season_last} were exactly the minimum support price, and simply predicting that price gave the lowest
error (\kn{mae_msp_mean} Indian Rupees per quintal). Basmati has no support price. Eight forecasting methods were compared on it under a
protocol frozen before testing, and on the held-out season none of them beat the naive forecast at 1, 7 or 14 days once multiple
comparisons were accounted for. An exact fair-allocation model raised the smallest share sold by any farmer from
\kn{allpol_p1_nearest_min_fulfilment_pct}\% under the nearest-mandi rule to \kn{allpol_kisaniti_min_fulfilment_pct}\%, across
\kn{allpol_plans} synthetic plans priced with real market data, at a cost of \kn{allpol_kisaniti_price_of_fairness_rs_per_q} Indian Rupees
per quintal. The complete system passed \kn{sys_tests_total} automated tests and a \kn{sys_chain_steps}-step end-to-end run on fictional
organization data.
\keywords{Farmer Producer Organizations \and Mandi price forecasting \and Minimum support price \and Fair allocation \and Integer
programming}
\end{abstract}
