\begin{abstract}
\draftfor{Drafted by Claude from verified results only; the authors rewrite the abstract in their own words.}
A Farmer Producer Organization (FPO) has to decide where, when and how much of its members' produce is sold,
and the tools that exist treat forecasting, storage and logistics separately. KisanNiti connects them in five
stages that an FPO manager follows: collecting and cleaning prices and farmer records, proposing a selling
plan, reviewing it, telling each farmer in their own language, and recording what was sold. A regime test
separates price-bound from market crops. For Paddy (Common) in Punjab, at least \kn{msp_share_min}\% of the reported
prices in every season from \kn{season_first} to \kn{season_last} equal the minimum support price, and a rule that
predicts the support price had the lowest error (Rs~\kn{mae_msp_mean} per quintal); no learned model beat it.
For basmati, whose prices move, a forecast comparison of eight methods was fixed in a pre-registered protocol
before any test result existed. On the held-out season, none of \kn{inf_total} comparisons with the naive forecast at 1, 7 and 14 days
found a method better after correcting for multiple comparisons; the one clearly different method, a seasonal naive rule, was worse.
An exact fair-allocation model shares each mandi's daily limit among members: in \kn{allpol_plans} synthetic
plans at real prices, every plan was valid and the smallest share a farmer received rose from
\kn{allpol_p1_nearest_min_fulfilment_pct}\% under the nearest-mandi rule to \kn{allpol_kisaniti_min_fulfilment_pct}\%, at a cost of
Rs~\kn{allpol_kisaniti_price_of_fairness_rs_per_q} per quintal. The system was built and tested end to end
(\kn{sys_tests_total} automated tests, a \kn{sys_chain_steps}-step run on fictional data); no real farmer data or user study is
included.
\keywords{Farmer Producer Organizations \and Mandi price forecasting \and
Minimum support price \and Fair allocation \and Integer programming \and Internet of Things}
\end{abstract}
