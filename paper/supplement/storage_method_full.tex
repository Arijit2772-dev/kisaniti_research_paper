\subsection{Storage Sensing and Risk Rule}
\label{sec:method-storage}

Basmati paddy is stored on the farm in jute bags, often for months. One protected SHT30 temperature and humidity probe
(typical tolerance $\pm2$\,\%RH between 20 and 80\,\%RH~\cite{sht3x}) is placed inside a bag and read every five minutes by an
ESP32 board~\cite{esp32} outside it (Fig.~\ref{fig:hardware}). The firmware checks the sensor's CRC, never sends a failed read
as zero, and numbers every message so that a repeated delivery is recognised. Messages travel over the farm's phone
hotspot by MQTT with TLS and a separate password and topic permission per device; a reading stays in a 288-sample (24\,h)
buffer until the server confirms that it has stored it, once per (device, boot, sequence). The same firmware runs in the
Wokwi simulator~\cite{wokwi}; readings from that twin are labelled synthetic.

Grain exchanges moisture with the air between its kernels until the two balance, and that air's relative humidity divided
by 100 is its water activity $a_w$. Storage fungi need at least 65\,\% relative humidity~\cite{gwinner1996}, and grain
kept below 65 to 70\,\% degrades slowly~\cite{delucia1994}. The rule takes the median humidity and temperature of the last
24\,h and returns LOW for $a_w<0.65$, MEDIUM for $0.65\le a_w<0.70$ and HIGH for $a_w\ge0.70$. The level rises as soon as the
median crosses a limit and falls only after it has stayed 2 points below the limit for 48\,h. Cold grain
($<10\,^\circ$C) moves one level down; warm, damp grain ($\ge25\,^\circ$C, $a_w\ge0.60$) moves from LOW to MEDIUM. The rule returns
UNKNOWN, with reasons, when the probe is registered as hanging in shed air, in the first 6\,h after insertion, when the newest
reading is older than 15 minutes, or when fewer than 75\,\% of the expected readings are usable. It does not report moisture
content and does not detect spoilage; it says whether the air in one bag favours mould and insects. The 6-hour settling
time and the 2-point margin are desk-research assumptions.
